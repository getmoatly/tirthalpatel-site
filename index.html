How I decide what gets AI

Not every bottleneck needs an agent. This is the intake path I set up at DaVita and use on my own product: score the request first, then send it to the cheapest approach that will hold up.

IntakeA team brings a bottleneck and what success looks like
Feasibility scorecardValue, data readiness, risk, and whether the task is deterministic or probabilistic
DeterministicRules or existing SaaS automation. Cheaper and fully auditable.
Agentic AILLM with retrieval, plus a human reviewing anything high-stakes
Run and measureObservability, ROI tracking, and a spec engineering can build against

The scorecard is what keeps capital going to the requests that need judgment, while everything else gets the plain automation it deserves.

How I manage complex technical programs

My delivery model follows PMI's Standard for Program Management: define the program, deliver it, then close it out. I add Lean Six Sigma measurement at both ends, so every program starts from a baseline and ends with benefits someone can verify.

Program definitionAlign and baselineBusiness case, OKRs, benefits map and today's metrics. Charter and roadmap agreed with executive sponsors.
Governance frameworkSet governanceSteering cadence, stage gates, decision rights, RACI and a single RAID log.
Program deliveryDeliver as a team of teamsWorkstreams planned together (Agile, SAFe or hybrid), with one dependency map and an integrated schedule and budget.
Monitoring and controllingMeasure and adjustKPI and OKR scorecards, risk reviews, change control and executive reporting tied to the baseline.
Program closureTransition and sustainHand over to operations, confirm benefits against the baseline, and capture lessons for the next program.
Running through every phase:
  • Stakeholder engagement
  • Benefits management
  • Risk
  • Collaboration
  • Strategic alignment

Each workstream inside the program runs through PMBOK's focus areas: initiating, planning, implementing, monitoring and controlling, and closing. My job is keeping those workstreams pointed at the same benefits.

An app I designed, built and shipped alone

The clearest evidence of how I run a program is one where I held every role: product manager, engineer, QA and marketer.

Moatly is a value-investing app for iOS and Android. It walks an investor through four questions about a business, then shows a value range and an AI mentor that knows their watchlist. The idea came from my own investing, and it is live on the App Store and Google Play.

MeaningDo I understand this business, and does it fit me?
MoatIs its advantage durable, measured over 1 to 10 years?
ManagementDo returns on capital and debt show good stewardship?
Margin of SafetyIs today's price far enough below what it's worth?

Getting through the release gate

App Store review rejected an early build because paid features didn't unlock after purchase. I traced it to a race between the app's page load and the billing webhook, made the billing SDK the single source of truth, and the next build was approved.

Regression discipline

Every change to the valuation engine is checked against a fixed set of real companies chosen to cover normal earnings, negative earnings and cash-flow-only cases, in development and again in the release build.

Code for math, AI for judgment

All valuation math runs in deterministic code. Claude only interprets results it is handed. It's the same split I apply to enterprise AI intake.

Owning go-to-market

I ran launch marketing, app-store optimization and a stock-teardown content series. The first case study on r/ValueInvesting drew more than 12,000 views.
